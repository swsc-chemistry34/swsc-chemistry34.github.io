Finished the Core work? Try Extend

Respiration releases heat. Photosynthesis takes it in. Explain why these two reactions are linked by energy.

Show a possible answer

Photosynthesis stores light energy in glucose. Respiration releases the same amount of energy when glucose reacts with oxygen. Energy is conserved, so the reverse of an exothermic reaction is endothermic.

Energy in reactions

Part 1 of 5

Enthalpy change

Enthalpy change, ΔH. The heat energy released or absorbed by a reaction at constant pressure. It is measured in kJ mol-1.

The reacting chemicals are the system. Everything around them is the surroundings. Heat moves between the two.

The sign of ΔH is from the point of view of the chemicals. Heat leaving the chemicals is negative.

Part 2 of 5

Energy level diagrams

An energy level diagram compares the energy of the reactants with the energy of the products.

If the products are lower, energy was released, so ΔH is negative. If the products are higher, energy was absorbed, so ΔH is positive.

Part 3 of 5

Why heat is released or absorbed

Chemical bonds store energy. A reaction breaks the bonds in the reactants and makes new bonds in the products.

Burning a fuel is exothermic. The bonds made in carbon dioxide and water release more energy than the bonds broken in the fuel and oxygen.

Part 4 of 5

Thermochemical equations

Thermochemical equation. A balanced equation with state symbols and the value of ΔH for the amounts shown.
CH4(g) + 2O2(g) → CO2(g) + 2H2O(l)   ΔH = −890 kJ mol-1
This means 890 kJ is released when 1 mol of methane burns completely.

The energy scales with the amount of fuel. Reversing a reaction changes the sign of ΔH.

Worked example

How much energy is released when 2.0 mol of methane burns completely?

Energy = 2.0 × 890 = 1780 kJ
Each mole releases 890 kJ, so 2.0 mol releases twice as much.
Part 5 of 5

Combustion

Combustion. A reaction in which a fuel reacts with oxygen and releases heat. It is always exothermic.
Complete combustionIncomplete combustion
Oxygen supplyPlentyLimited
Products of burning methaneCO2 and H2OCO or C as well as H2O
FlameBlueYellow and sooty
Energy releasedThe mostLess
2CH4(g) + 3O2(g) → 2CO(g) + 4H2O(l)
Check the balance. Each side has 2 carbon, 8 hydrogen and 6 oxygen atoms.

Carbon monoxide, CO, is a poisonous gas. Soot, C, is unburnt carbon. Both still hold energy that was not released.

Finished the Core work? Try Extend

Burning methane to make liquid water gives ΔH = −890 kJ mol-1. Making water vapour gives ΔH = −802 kJ mol-1. Suggest why the values differ.

Show the answer

Turning water vapour into liquid water releases extra energy, because bonds form between the water molecules. The equation makes 2 mol of water, and 2 × 44 = 88 kJ. This is the gap between 890 and 802.

Is it complete?

Burning is useful, and it can be dangerous. Pick a setting. Decide whether the combustion is complete or incomplete, and give two reasons.

Worked example

Setting: A Bunsen burner has its air hole closed. The flame is yellow and leaves soot on a beaker.

Choice: Incomplete combustion. Less oxygen reaches the gas, so some carbon is left as soot, C.

The fix is to open the air hole. The flame turns blue and burns completely.

Incomplete combustion is dangerous and also wasteful. Explain why it is wasteful.

Key words

Enthalpy change (ΔH)
The heat energy released or absorbed by a reaction at constant pressure, in kJ mol⁻¹.
Exothermic
A reaction that releases heat to the surroundings. ΔH is negative.
Endothermic
A reaction that absorbs heat from the surroundings. ΔH is positive.
System
The reacting chemicals.
Surroundings
Everything around the reacting chemicals.
Energy level diagram
A diagram that compares the energy of the reactants and the products.
Thermochemical equation
A balanced equation with state symbols and the value of ΔH.
Combustion
A reaction in which a fuel reacts with oxygen and releases heat.
Complete combustion
Combustion with plenty of oxygen. It makes carbon dioxide and water.
Incomplete combustion
Combustion with limited oxygen. It makes carbon monoxide or soot.
Watch out

A negative ΔH does not mean less energy was used. It means energy was released.

Watch out

An exothermic reaction can still need a spark or a flame to start.

Watch out

The sign of ΔH is from the point of view of the chemicals, not the surroundings.

Watch out

Blue flames show complete combustion. Yellow sooty flames show incomplete combustion.
