Finished the Core work? Try Extend

Protein is mostly used to build and repair the body. Why would your body burn protein for energy only when other fuel runs low?

Show a possible answer

Proteins make muscle, enzymes and many other parts of the body. Burning them for energy uses up molecules that have other jobs. Carbohydrates and fats are the body's main fuels, so they are used first.

How food gives energy

Part 1 of 5

Food is a fuel

Nutrient. A substance in food that your body uses for energy, growth or repair.

Your body gets energy from three groups of nutrients. Each group is made of large molecules.

The energy is stored in the chemical bonds, just as it is in a fuel such as petrol.

Part 2 of 5

Energy content

Energy content. The energy released when a given mass of a food burns or is respired. It is measured in kJ g-1, kilojoules per gram.
NutrientEnergy content (kJ g-1)
Carbohydrate17
Protein17
Fat37

These are the average values used on Australian food labels.

To find the energy in a food, multiply each mass by its energy content. Then add.

Worked example

A snack bar has 20 g of carbohydrate, 8 g of fat and 4 g of protein.

Carbohydrate: 20 × 17 = 340 kJ
Fat: 8 × 37 = 296 kJ
Protein: 4 × 17 = 68 kJ
Total: 340 + 296 + 68 = 704 kJ
Each mass in grams times kJ g-1 gives kilojoules. The grams cancel.
Part 3 of 5

Why does fat have more energy?

A gram of fat gives more than twice the energy of a gram of carbohydrate. The reason is the oxygen in each molecule.

Burning joins carbon and hydrogen to oxygen. Glucose already holds a lot of oxygen, so it is part-way to burnt. A fatty acid holds very little, so each gram has more left to give.

This is a simple model. The energy comes from the bonds that break and form, and the oxygen share is a good guide to how much is left.

Part 4 of 5

Digestion

Digestion. The breakdown of large food molecules into small molecules that your body can absorb.

Glucose is the main fuel your cells use. The body can also make it from the products of fats and proteins.

Part 5 of 5

Respiration

Cellular respiration. The reaction in which cells react glucose with oxygen to release energy.
C6H12O6(aq) + 6O2(g) → 6CO2(g) + 6H2O(l)
Check the balance. Each side has 6 carbon, 12 hydrogen and 18 oxygen atoms.

This is the same equation as burning glucose. In a cell, the reaction happens in many small steps, so the energy comes out gently and the cell can capture it.

Finished the Core work? Try Extend

Fermentation and respiration both start with glucose. Name one product they share and one way they differ.

Show a possible answer

Shared: both make carbon dioxide.

Different: respiration uses oxygen and ends with carbon dioxide and water, so it releases much more energy. Fermentation makes ethanol, which still holds energy and can be burned as a fuel.

Choose the food

The best food depends on the job. Pick a setting, choose an option, and give two reasons.

Worked example

Setting: A student has an exam in 30 minutes and is hungry.

Choice: A banana. Carbohydrate digests fairly quickly to glucose, which the blood carries to the brain and muscles.

The cost is that a banana has less energy per gram than a fatty food. Chips would digest slowly and could leave the student feeling heavy.

Finished the Core work? Try Extend

An apple gives only about 2 kJ for each gram, far less than the 17 kJ g-1 for carbohydrate. Suggest why.

Show a possible answer

The 17 kJ g-1 value is for the carbohydrate itself. Most of an apple is water, which gives no energy, so only a small part of each gram is carbohydrate.

Calculate the energy

A snack has 25 g of carbohydrate, 12 g of fat and 6 g of protein. Find its energy content in kJ.

Show the answer

Carbohydrate: 25 × 17 = 425 kJ

Fat: 12 × 37 = 444 kJ

Protein: 6 × 17 = 102 kJ

Total: 425 + 444 + 102 = 971 kJ

Key words

Nutrient
A substance in food that your body uses for energy, growth or repair.
Carbohydrate
A nutrient such as starch or sugar. Digestion breaks it into glucose.
Fat
A nutrient such as butter or oil. Digestion breaks it into fatty acids and glycerol.
Protein
A nutrient made of amino acids. Digestion breaks it into amino acids.
Energy content
The energy released from one gram of a food, in kJ g⁻¹.
Digestion
The breakdown of large food molecules into small molecules your body can absorb.
Glucose
A sugar with the formula C₆H₁₂O₆. It is the main fuel your cells use.
Cellular respiration
The reaction in which cells react glucose with oxygen to release energy.
Fatty acid
A small molecule, mostly carbon and hydrogen, made when fat is digested.
Amino acid
A small molecule made when protein is digested.
Watch out

Food energy is not made by your body. It was stored from sunlight by plants.

Watch out

Gram for gram, fat gives more than twice the energy of carbohydrate or protein.

Watch out

The energy content values are for the pure nutrient, not for the whole food. Water and fibre give no energy.

Watch out

Respiration is the same reaction as burning glucose, but it happens in many small steps inside cells.
