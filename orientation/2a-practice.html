Worked examples

Part 1 of 5

The method

Energy released. The amount of fuel that burns, in mol, multiplied by the energy released by each mole.

A thermochemical equation such as CH4(g) + 2O2(g) → CO2(g) + 2H2O(l), ΔH = −890 kJ mol-1 tells you that each mole of methane releases 890 kJ.

Energy released = n × 890
n is the amount of methane in mol. 890 is the energy released by 1 mol, in kJ mol-1.

Every question follows the same path.

  1. Change what you are given into moles of fuel.
  2. Multiply by the energy released by each mole.
  3. Give the answer in kJ with the right significant figures.
Part 2 of 5

From a mass of fuel to energy

Worked example

How much energy is released when 23.0 g of ethanol burns completely? ΔH = −1367 kJ mol-1.

n(C2H5OH) = 23.046.0 = 0.500 mol
M(C2H5OH) = (2 × 12.0) + (6 × 1.0) + 16.0 = 46.0 g mol-1.
Energy released = 0.500 × 1367 = 684 kJ
Each mole releases 1367 kJ, so 0.500 mol releases half of that.
Part 3 of 5

From a gas volume to energy

Worked example

How much energy is released when 49.6 L of methane at SLC burns completely?

n(CH4) = 49.624.8 = 2.00 mol
At SLC, 1 mol of any gas has a volume of 24.8 L.
Energy released = 2.00 × 890 = 1780 kJ

Part 5 of 5

Carbon dioxide made

The same moles of fuel also tell you how much carbon dioxide forms. Use the mole ratio from the equation.

Worked example

What mass of carbon dioxide forms when 2.00 mol of methane burns completely?

CH4(g) + 2O2(g) → CO2(g) + 2H2O(l)
n(CO2) = 2.00 mol     m(CO2) = 2.00 × 44.0 = 88.0 g
The ratio of CH4 to CO2 is 1 to 1. The energy released at the same time is 2.00 × 890 = 1780 kJ.

You can now compare fuels. Methane makes 44.0 g of carbon dioxide for each 890 kJ.

Methane: 44.0890 × 1000 = 49.4 g of CO2 for each 1000 kJ
Ethanol: 88.01367 × 1000 = 64.4 g of CO2 for each 1000 kJ
Ethanol makes 2 mol of CO2 for each mole burnt. Methane makes more energy for each gram of carbon dioxide.

A hydrogen fuel cell car and a petrol car store the same mass of fuel. Use the energy for each gram to explain why hydrogen is attractive, and give one reason it is hard to use.

Show a possible answer

Hydrogen releases about 143 kJ for each gram, which is more than methane (55.6 kJ g-1) or ethanol (29.7 kJ g-1). It also makes only water when it burns or reacts in a fuel cell.

It is hard to use because hydrogen is a gas with a very low density, so it needs a large volume or very high pressure to store each gram.

Key words

Enthalpy change, ΔH
The heat energy released or absorbed by a reaction. For combustion it is negative, in kJ mol⁻¹.
Thermochemical equation
A balanced equation with state symbols and the value of ΔH.
Amount, n
The number of moles of a substance, in mol.
Molar mass, M
The mass of 1 mol of a substance, in g mol⁻¹.
Molar volume
The volume of 1 mol of any gas. At SLC it is 24.8 L mol⁻¹.
Energy for each gram
The energy released by 1 g of fuel, in kJ g⁻¹. It equals the energy for each mole divided by the molar mass.
Greenhouse gas
A gas such as carbon dioxide or methane that traps heat in the atmosphere.
Watch out

A negative ΔH tells you energy is released. Use its size, not its sign, when you state the energy released.

Watch out

Always change the mass into moles before you multiply by ΔH. ΔH is for each mole, not for each gram.

Watch out

Use the molar volume 24.8 L mol⁻¹ only for gases at SLC. Liquid fuels such as ethanol are measured by mass.

Watch out

Give the answer with the same number of significant figures as the least precise data.
